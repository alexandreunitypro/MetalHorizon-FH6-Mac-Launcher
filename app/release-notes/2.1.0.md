**MetalHorizon X 2.1: your add-ons, your VIP and your profile picture recognised, PlayStation pads on Bluetooth, and MetalDuck's frame generation built in.** MetalHorizon updates itself to this version when it opens, and installs Blackwell Engine 1.3 by itself.

### Your add-ons, your VIP and your picture (issue #18)
- **What you own is owned in the game.** The in-game store priced add-ons you had bought, and the VIP membership was not seen: the game asked what the account owns and always heard "nothing". MetalHorizon now reads your account's Store collection and answers it. Add-ons you bought count, and so does everything an edition or bundle you own contains: the VIP membership and the Car Pass of a Premium or Deluxe Edition count even when the Store does not list them one by one. The game's entitled products and its add-on licences (the way it checks VIP) come from the same answer.
- **Your own profile picture**, from your Xbox profile, in each size the game asks for, instead of a grey square.
- When Microsoft cannot be reached, the game gets what it got before (the game itself, no add-on, the grey picture) and never an error, so nothing here can stop it from starting.
- Checked without the game: the Xbox runtime of Blackwell Engine 1.3 was asked the game's own questions, with answers made from the live Store catalog for an account owning the Deluxe Edition and some add-ons. All eight owned add-ons were marked owned, including the two the Deluxe Edition holds, and each got a licence. The profile picture came back at its four sizes. This was checked on the Rosetta engine and on the native one (`engine/tests/store-ownership`).

### PlayStation pads, on Bluetooth too (issue #15)
- A pad is now what Windows makes of one: a gamepad, and also a generic controller and a menu-navigation device. A game that looks for either, as a racing game that also takes wheels may, finds it.
- A **DualSense on Bluetooth** never reached Windows' XInput under Wine. When XInput has no pad, the engines now read the pads macOS itself knows, through Apple's GameController framework: DualSense, DualShock 4, Xbox, Switch Pro and MFi pads, on Bluetooth as on a cable. On a cable, nothing changes: XInput still reads the pad first.
- Checked without the game against a simulated DualSense: found, read as a gamepad, in the menus and as a generic controller, its rumble passed on, and reported gone once unplugged. This was checked on Blackwell Engine 1.3, Steam 1.3 and Native 1.3 (`engine/tests/gameinput-pads`). The Bluetooth path itself could not be tried with a real pad. Please tell us in issue #15.

### Frame generation, from MetalDuck
- **Settings › Graphics › Frame generation (MetalDuck)**: 2×, 3× or 4× the frames on screen. It is MetalDuck's technique, by Roberto Camargo, built into MetalHorizon:
  - MetalHorizon finds the game's window by itself and captures it;
  - Apple's on-device model (VideoToolbox, macOS 26) makes the frames in between;
  - the frames are shown over the game, click-through.
- It starts and stops with the game, works with every engine (D3DMetal, Native, Beta) and both editions, and needs no separate app.
- **Automatic** interpolates at the largest size your Mac's model takes: macOS 27 tells it, 1920×1080 on an M5 Pro, at about 9 ms a frame. With **Frame cap** on None, the game is capped at your screen's refresh divided by the multiplier (30 fps at 2× on a 60 Hz screen), so its frames come evenly.
- It costs about one frame of latency. The first time, macOS asks you to let MetalHorizon record the screen, so that it can see the game's window. Needs macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon.

### The download says when it could not happen
A download that stopped without the game, for example on a disk without room for its 171 GB, no longer says "Download finished.". MetalHorizon checks the free space before a first download, and after any download it checks that the game is really there. When it is not, it says why.

### Beta engine 1.4a1 (Settings › Advanced › Beta engine)
- It is built on Blackwell Engine 1.3, with everything above, plus the beta's work against the Rosetta hitches.
- **Blackwell Trap Boost.** Forza Horizon 6's copy protection executes waves of deliberately invalid instructions. Rosetta's exception server handles each one, and at default priority it waits for a core. The beta raises that one thread, only in the game and only under Rosetta. Measured without the game on an M5 Pro, against busy high-priority threads: 108 µs a trap without it, 60 µs with it. Mathias Kowoll (MacGameFix) found the cause.
- The game sees at most 8 processors (on a Mac with more), as MacGameFix recommends.
- The **Blackwell Pacer** tunes itself between 10 and 60 % of the time.
- It also carries Highball's five Rosetta-line patches.

### Known limits
- None of the new answers to the game (add-ons, VIP, picture, pads) could be tried in the game itself: they were checked against the game's questions, without it. Please send Activity › Export Diagnostics if something is off.
- Frame generation was checked on Apple's model and on its settings, not over the game itself.
- The detailed messages (diagnostics, repairs, the Doctor's checks) are in English for now, and so are the new frame generation settings.
- The app is ad-hoc signed, not notarized yet: macOS asks you to confirm the first launch.
